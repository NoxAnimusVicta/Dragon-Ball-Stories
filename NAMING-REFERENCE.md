# Dragon Ball naming reference

Accepted campaign naming direction · 18 October 2026

This is the dedicated reference for creating names in this campaign. Consult it before proposing character, species, place, item or technique names. It is separate from the narrator and dialogue guide.

## Start with something ordinary

Begin with a familiar food, vegetable, household object, material, everyday word or plain description. Choose the underlying word or theme before trying to make the name sound impressive. A simple name can belong to an imposing character; the character does not need an elaborate fantasy title to carry their presence.

Keep the source word unchanged when it already works. Otherwise try a small spelling or sound change, remove a syllable, shorten the ending, or make a light pun. Preserve an audible connection to the source instead of transforming it beyond recognition. Related characters may share a source category without all sounding alike.

The player's reference examples are the food/vegetable associations behind Cereal, Granolah, Vegeta, Caulifla and Raditz. These illustrate the desired creative approach; this document is a campaign style reference, not a claim that every name follows a literal English letter-substitution formula.

## Techniques and practical magic

Use a clear description of what the technique does when that is enough. Short, concrete words should be the starting point. Add flair when it suits the technique and its creator, rather than automatically combining grand or mysterious words.

**Space Barrier** is the accepted naming example: straightforward and functional. **Void Veil** was rejected as generic fantasy styling. The name alone does not grant a character a technique; ability records own acquisition and mechanics.

## A short naming process

1. Identify the appropriate everyday source or existing group theme.
2. Try the plain word first, then a few small variations.
3. Say the candidates aloud and check that they are memorable and easy to use in dialogue.
4. Check existing names for collisions and the relevant species/family convention.
5. Offer a short selection with the source word explained; distinguish proposals from accepted names.

Do not impose a food pun on every technique or rename already accepted names merely to enforce this preference. More ceremonial names can still be used when specifically wanted; everyday roots are the default starting point.

## Authorship and attribution

**Rivet and Brask are player-approved examples of successful assistant-proposed names.** They share an industrial theme, sound distinct from one another, and follow the requested approach without elaborate fantasy wording. Rivet uses an ordinary hardware word unchanged; Brask complements the pair's industrial sound. No specific source-word derivation for Brask is asserted here. Use this pair as a practical benchmark for future suggestions: a simple foundation, a coherent theme and only as much alteration as the name needs.

**Auber, derived from aubergine, was created and named by the player before the assistant had any input.** It illustrates the player's naming approach, not an assistant naming success. Later assistant development does not transfer authorship of an existing character or name.

Record who supplied a name and its intended wordplay when known. Do not present player-created work as an assistant invention. Keep unrevealed biography, powers and future plans out of this public naming reference.
