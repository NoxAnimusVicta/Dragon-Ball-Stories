# Saladverse and infinite timelines

Campaign agreement recorded 13 October 2026. This is setting continuity, not a claim about official Dragon Ball canon or a mathematical proof. It advances no scene, character knowledge, power level or story clock.

## Infinite histories and counterparts

The campaign contains infinitely many timelines realising every possible history, with infinitely many compatible variations of each realised outcome. An unusual outcome is not a unique outcome. Similar timelines can share a character's entire known life while differing elsewhere, or give that character substantially different experiences and choices.

Azeer, Frieza's human reincarnation in Salad Saiyan's story, therefore has infinitely many timeline counterparts and possible histories. The video follows a particular counterpart; it does not establish the only possible Azeer. A fate shown for that individual is not a universal fate imposed on every counterpart.

This does not make counterparts interchangeable. Each has an individual identity, source history, relationships and personally experienced past. Meeting another counterpart does not resurrect someone who died, transfer their relationships, or undo their death. Recruiting people from genuinely different timelines is distinct from extracting the same person repeatedly from different dates of one history; the latter remains a destructive temporal crime under the existing rules.

Conton City's protected present and the unique Supreme Kai of Time remain the established exceptions to ordinary timeline replication. Infinite possible native histories do not create copies of Conton, rewind a resident's external past or reveal a traveller's unwritten future. Availability of counterparts does not guarantee that the Patrol can locate, reach or recruit any desired individual.

## Natural and forced branching

Fixed points allow safe natural branching into possible histories. This natural diversity is part of the setting, not an integrity emergency and not evidence that every timeline was created by a time machine. The Patrol does not enforce a single Prime history.

Natural branching is distinct from an outside intervention breaking an established fixed point or exhausting a timeline's integrity. Those forced branches retain the existing consequences: normally both histories return to 100% current integrity but lose underlying structural stability. Repeated forced splits increase anomaly risk. Existing native-participant latitude, the specific Future Trunks exception and precision-extraction rules still apply. The safe natural process does not grant intruders permission to force the same outcome without damage.

## Saladverse integration

Salad Saiyan's what-if histories are incorporated into the campaign's wider timeline landscape. Their timelines are assumed to have run their native courses; they are not awaiting progression alongside Zero's local story. The Cell Prime storyline is currently unfolding in the Time Patrol's shared external present. No exact episode position or new encounter with Zero has been assigned.

- Read the source's **continuity** as a campaign **timeline**, with its related timelines and branches where relevant; no additional supervisory layer is introduced.
- Replace the **Continuity Patrol** with the existing **Time Patrol** under Chronoa. Its personnel belong to this institution, not a second competing Patrol.
- Preserve the stories' characters, relationships, transformations and events by default, including Azeer's later Cell Prime involvement. Do not silently discard a difficult fate or restrict him to his original finale.
- Where a specific source event conflicts with an established campaign rule, record and reconcile that concrete conflict. Do not reject the entire history or invent a second cosmology pre-emptively.

Keep each source counterpart distinct from possible variants we later introduce. A new variant needs its own identity and history; it must not silently replace the individual depicted in the source. The active Cell Prime sequence follows external time, so its later events are not automatically completed or readable merely because native timelines can be viewed as historical courses.
